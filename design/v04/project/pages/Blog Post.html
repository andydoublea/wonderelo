Meeting points: the secret ingredient of great networking rounds

Why designated meeting spots make networking less awkward, more efficient, and way more fun for everyone involved — with practical setups from three real events.

What are meeting points?

Meeting points are designated spots where matched participants meet for their networking conversation. Instead of wandering around looking for each other, both people know exactly where to go — Table 3, the Corner Lounge, or the Blue Flag area.

The idea sounds almost too simple to write down. And yet, of all the things we've changed about networking rounds over the past two years, this is the one organizers tell us made the biggest difference.

"We thought the matching algorithm was the magic. Turns out the real magic was telling people exactly where to stand." Marek H. — co-organizer, Forum 2025

Why they matter more than you think

Without meeting points, matched participants waste precious minutes finding each other. In a crowded venue, this can eat up half the round time. With clear meeting points, conversations start immediately — and that compounds.

We measured this at three events of similar size:

+40%
Longer conversations. Events with clearly-named meeting points saw average per-pair talk time grow from 4 min 12 s to 5 min 54 s on a six-minute round.

How to set them up

Choose locations that are:

  • Easy to find — numbered tables, colored zones, or recognizable landmarks (the espresso bar, the merch wall).
  • Spread across the venue — avoid clustering everyone in one area; you'll create a bottleneck.
  • Conversation-friendly — away from loud speakers, the stage feed, and the main flow of foot traffic.

Most organizers get this right on the second event, never the first. If you only do one thing the first time around, walk the venue an hour before doors with the points marked on your phone and check the noise from each one.

Creative meeting-point ideas

Go beyond numbered tables. Use themed areas — "The Innovation Corner", "The Wonderelo Lounge" — or even outdoor spaces for a fresh-air networking experience. At one wedding we set up four meeting points named after the couple's favorite films. Guests posted photos with the signs all night.

The name does double duty: it tells participants where to go, and it tells them what kind of conversation they're walking into.

The result

Events with well-designed meeting points see longer conversations, higher follow-up rates, and — based on the post-event survey — noticeably higher reported enjoyment. When people don't waste time finding each other, they spend more time connecting. And that's what networking is all about.
